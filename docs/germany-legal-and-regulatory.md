# Germany: regulatory obligations, legal ownership and release controls

Scope: Germany, checked against primary legal and regulator sources on 16 October 2026. This is an implementation and counsel-review specification, not an opinion that Zackisch is legally compliant. Laws, guidance, customer/merchant contractual relationships and actual configuration must be re-verified before production launch and for any new region. Do not publish this engineering document as legal advice to restaurants. The legally definitive text requires review by German counsel and a tax adviser using Zackisch's actual company identity, tax status, Stripe agreement and operational workflows.

## 1. Map the contracting and payment parties first

Proposed product model, contingent on the actual legal structure:

1. **Guest ↔ restaurant (B2C):** restaurant is the seller of meals and drinks, determines menu/prices/allergens, prepares and fulfils orders, issues legally relevant sales/tax/fiscal receipts where applicable, and handles statutory customer rights and refunds; Zackisch supplies ordering software as an agent/service provider, not the restaurant of record.
2. **Restaurant ↔ Zackisch (B2B):** Zackisch sells access to ordering/payment software and charges the contractual usage-based platform fee; a written merchant SaaS contract must specify the precise fee base, VAT, refund treatment, payment processing, cancellation/pause, availability boundaries, data duties, support and allocation of responsibility for fiscal integration/manual POS entries.
3. **Merchant ↔ Stripe (payment services):** selected Connect model and account controller/liability model determine Stripe contractual terms, onboarding/KYC, bank verification, merchant of record, payment processing fees, reserves, negative balances, settlement, reports and disputes. Zackisch must not represent itself as Stripe or claim a payment-services exemption without review.
4. **Zackisch ↔ its processors/vendors:** host, database, mail, monitoring, possible analytics, contractor and financial vendors require a purpose/role/legal-basis classification and suitable contractual controls (including a DPA where the GDPR requires one).

**Hard blocker:** obtain a payment-services/ZAG assessment of the *actual* payment flow, not just a sales description. Avoid custody of guests' money or an in-house wallet. Changing Stripe from platform charges to direct charges changes payment object location, fees, settlement reports and incident response: do not perform as a cosmetic refactor. BaFin's ZAG guidance: https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Veroeffentlichungen/DE/Merkblatt/mb_111222_zag.html .

A privacy notice and a processing agreement are **not interchangeable**. A generic acceptance checkbox is not a lawful substitute for a genuine consent where consent is required. Accessibility statement/consumer accessibility information and a rights/contact channel should be reviewed under BFSG/BFSGV for the applicable service. Legal pages must work without a merchant login.

## 3. Consumer contract and guest ordering

- Check §312i and §312j BGB and relevant EGBGB information obligations; the commercial contract and on-premise vs distance sale situation determines applicability. For relevant electronic consumer contracts, place material characteristics, restaurant identity, total price including mandatory amounts, payment method and fulfilment terms **immediately before** the binding order; use a button such as `Zahlungspflichtig bestellen` only after counsel validates that it corresponds to the flow. Stripe's payment element/embedded checkout must not hide required notices behind an iframe or a second ambiguous button. Record accepted terms only where an actual contractual acknowledgement is needed; provide confirmation on a durable medium where required.
- Clarify cancellation/refund vs statutory withdrawal. §312g(2) BGB has exceptions for perishable/personalized goods and certain scheduled food services, but no blanket rule that all on-premise or takeaway orders lack withdrawal rights. Publish narrowly reviewed restaurant-specific refund conditions and customer service contacts.
- PAngV §§3, 6, 13: display VAT-inclusive final guest prices; restaurant price lists have additional in-premises requirements (including entrance display) **not fulfilled solely by displaying Zackisch's online menu**. Show mandatory fees and taxes correctly; do not make optional tip or donation a hidden default. Restaurant menus should accurately label quantities/options/size changes.
- LMIV Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 and German LMIDV §4: food allergen information must be available in the legally prescribed form, accurate and current; provide data fields for ingredients/allergens and a clearly visible pre-purchase presentation. Merchants are responsible for input, Zackisch for a reliable interface and faithful rendering. Add warnings against reliance on unverified auto-generated allergen information; review additives, alcohol/youth protection and special product categories as relevant.
- If consumers receive a sales receipt from Zackisch, it must not falsely be labelled a fully compliant fiscal cash register receipt. Define payer-facing document types and the separate restaurant's POS / TSE receipt process with counsel and tax specialists.

## 4. Fiscal scope: not a POS does not automatically mean outside fiscalization

AO §146a and KassenSichV (including §1, §6), GoBD and applicable financial-administration guidance require a **fact-specific** assessment of an ordering and payment system. The existing POS does not automatically neutralize potential obligations arising from Zackisch recording sales electronically. Before DE launch obtain a documented classification of the exact product and data exports, required timing of cash-register booking, TSE/DSFinV-K obligations if applicable, receipt format, audit trail and retention. If external POS manual entry is relied upon, test the reconciliation and operational feasibility and document what happens during payment success but tablet/internet outage. Never promise that Zackisch is 'TSE-free', 'legally exempt', a replacement invoice or tax-certified without formal evidence.

## 5. Fees, invoices and VAT

- Restaurant's food/drink turnover is **not Zackisch's turnover**. Zackisch's B2B consideration is the contracted platform fee; platform payment settlement must reconcile gross B2C amount, taxes allocated to the restaurant, processor fees, Zackisch fee, refunds/chargebacks, balance impact and payout separately.
- German restaurant and catering food services generally use 7% VAT from 1 January 2026 while drinks generally use 19%; eligibility and mixed bundles require item/category classification and tax review. Restaurant-specific exemption and §19 UStG small-business choices must be modelled when applicable, without letting merchants arbitrarily choose a tax rate that violates law.
- Zackisch's **own** domestic B2B platform service will generally be subject to the standard German VAT rate (often 19% for a taxable Germany-based service), but the company's tax registration, small-business status, recipient place of supply, fee taxation and partner services must be signed off by tax counsel. Do not assert that a 2% gross-base platform fee automatically includes or excludes 19% VAT: display the commercial fee and VAT treatment precisely according to contract.
- E-invoicing: German businesses must be able to receive compliant E-invoices since 1 January 2025. In the domestic B2B context the transition to mandatory issuance ends generally after 2026, with certain issuers eligible for transition until end 2027. Simple PDFs are not, by themselves, compliant structured E-invoices. Build a tax-compliant issuer/recipient workflow (for example validated EN 16931 XRechnung or ZUGFeRD appropriate profile), deliver an immutable copy and retain necessary accounting records. BMF FAQ as of March 2026: https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/FAQ/e-rechnung.html .
- §14b UStG currently provides an eight-year invoice retention period; §147 AO generally differentiates 10/8/6 years by category, plus extensions when tax matters remain open. Do not convert these into one global personal-data retention time for *all* guests and tickets. See `tax-invoicing-and-fiscalization.md` and `privacy-and-data-governance.md`.

## 6. GDPR, cookies, communications, employees

Follow GDPR Arts. 5, 6, 13–15, 17, 21, 28, 30, 32–35, 44 ff., BDSG and TDDDG §25. Determine controller/processor status per processing purpose: Zackisch can be processor for restaurant-controlled order details while independently controlling account security, B2B billing, abuse prevention and its own marketing. Stripe may have separate or mixed roles under its contracts. Create a processing-register with purposes, data subjects, legal basis, recipients, sub-processors, locations/transfer mechanisms, deletion and TOMs; implement DPA and vendor notices where necessary. Sensitive or unnecessary guest data should not be collected. Cookies for strictly necessary session/service functions may satisfy the §25(2) TDDDG exception; optional analytics/advertising require appropriate prior consent and equivalent rejection. A truthful cookie inventory must be generated from the deployed product, not marketing boilerplate. Handle DSAR, breach response (including Art. 33 time frames when applicable), business deletion, ledger preservation and backup retention. For newsletter email marketing evaluate UWG §7 and documented double opt-in; transactional emails are not marketing permission. See the dedicated privacy document.

## 7. Accessibility and advertising boundaries

BFSG/BFSGV applicability to consumer electronic commerce must be checked separately for Zackisch and merchants. The microenterprise exception for services exists but does not prove that a particular operator is exempt. Meet WCAG 2.2 AA as Zackisch's design-and-test objective. Do not claim legal certification based on passing an automated checker. No misleading '100% secure', guaranteed uptime, zero-cost-total-operation, automatic POS compliance or guaranteed revenue outcomes. Security positioning is tertiary and factual: role-protected data, verified backups and ability to keep using the existing POS while Zackisch is unavailable. Statements must match observed controls.

## 8. Related jurisdiction-specific risks requiring a written yes/no decision

- P2B Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 applicability to online intermediation: assess whether Zackisch facilitates direct B2C transactions in a way triggering trader terms, ranking/suspension transparency, internal complaint handling and dispute obligations.
- Digital Services Act, DAC7 platform operator reporting, packaging-related rules, consumer dispute information, youth protection for restricted goods and GDPR international transfers: perform scope analysis, do not automatically claim non-applicability. Consumer OS/ODR platform changed in 2025: never paste obsolete online dispute resolution links without checking current requirements.
- Cyber/security and NIS2 requirements require a size/service-scope determination, rather than an unsupported compliance claim.
